Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Waukegan, IL

Drywall and wall cavities hide water well. A bubble in paint or a stain at the baseboard can mean a wet cavity behind it.

(847) 368-7353

Wall and drywall water damage repair in Waukegan covers drying wall cavities, removing drywall that cannot be saved and rebuilding the wall after a leak. Water inside a wall is hard to see and slow to dry, so the repair starts with finding it. If you notice a soft spot, bubbling paint or a stain at the baseboard, call dispatch.

What a wet wall looks like

Drywall wicks water upward and outward from the source. A wet wall may show a stain at the baseboard, paint that bubbles, wallpaper that lifts, a soft spot when you press it or a musty smell. A pipe in the wall, a siding leak, a window failure or water from a room above can all put water in the same wall.

Some homeowners find a large hole or a rotted section only after a kitchen wall begins to fail from a siding leak on the outside. In that case the repair involves the exterior and the interior, and the exterior trade is a siding or roofing contractor.

How wet drywall is handled

Crews use a moisture meter and thermal camera to map the wet area, then decide between drying in place and cutting out. Clean-water drywall that is only damp near the bottom can sometimes be dried with air movers and wall cavity drying systems. Drywall that is saturated, soft or discolored is cut out above the wet line, and wet insulation is removed.

Cutting an opening lets the cavity dry quickly. The method follows structural drying. If the stain is on a ceiling, ceiling water damage repair covers it, and if the source was a kitchen leak, see kitchen water damage restoration.

  1. Find and stop the source with a plumber or the right trade.
  2. Map the wet area and remove material that cannot dry.
  3. Dry the cavity until readings match the dry standard.
  4. Replace insulation and drywall, tape, texture and paint.

Wall repair in Waukegan houses

Some older houses have plaster over wood lath instead of drywall, and the Near North Historic District has buildings from the 1840s to 1928, though your own walls may differ. Plaster can survive a wetting that would ruin drywall, but it can also detach from the lath. Matching historic trim and plaster takes a different repair approach, so mention the age of the house when you call.

Practical tie-back: if a wall was wet from a leak above, check the ceiling and the floor next to it. Water that ran down a wall may also be in the floor edge, which can lead to carpet water damage restoration or flooring work.

Sudden leaks that wet a wall are often covered as accidental water damage. Long-running leaks and wear often are not. Ask your insurer, and photograph the wall before it is opened.

  • Turn off power to a room if water is running behind an outlet or switch
  • Do not poke holes in a bulging wall, because the water can rush out
  • Move furniture away from the wall and photograph the stain

Frequently asked questions

Can drywall be saved after it gets wet?

Sometimes. Clean-water drywall that is only damp can dry in place. Drywall that is soft, saturated or discolored is usually cut out.

How high does drywall get cut after a leak?

Above the wet line, with a margin. Crews decide using moisture readings rather than a fixed height.

Why does the stain come back after I paint?

The wall may still hold moisture, or the leak may still be active. Paint over a wet wall traps the water.

Will you repair a plaster wall?

Wet plaster is assessed case by case. Some can be dried in place, and areas that detach are repaired by a plaster or finish specialist.

Is wall water damage covered by insurance?

Sudden leaks often are. Slow leaks and wear often are not. Ask your insurer.
