About Waukegan Water Damage Pros

We answer the phone at any hour and send a crew for water damage in Waukegan and nearby Lake County towns. This page explains how we work and what you can expect.

(847) 368-7353

Waukegan Water Damage Pros is a local dispatch and restoration service for water damage, flooding and mold. Our office is at 2603 Grand Avenue in Waukegan, and the phone line is answered 24 hours a day, every day of the year. We are licensed and insured.

How we work

Water damage is stressful, and the first call matters. The dispatcher asks a few direct questions, tells you what to do while the crew is on the way and explains what will happen next. When the crew arrives, they walk the affected areas and explain the scope and price before any work starts.

  • Dispatch is answered every hour of every day
  • Same-day visits when a crew is available
  • Scope and price are explained before work starts
  • Drying is checked with moisture readings, not guesses
  • Mold and sewage are handled with their own steps

What we do not do

We do not decide insurance coverage, and we do not promise results that depend on the building. We document the damage so you can report it. Your insurer decides coverage, and the building decides how long drying takes.

Where we work

Our home base is Waukegan, the Lake County seat on the Lake Michigan shoreline. We also serve Wadsworth and Lake Villa, and the service area page shows the full picture. Every service we offer is listed on the services page.

Straight talk about limits

Some problems are bigger than drying. A foundation that leaks every spring, a roof that fails or a sewer line that keeps clogging needs a specialist, and we will say so. Restoration handles the water that got in. It does not stop water from coming back.

We also tell you when a material is better replaced than dried. Saturated insulation, soft drywall and padding that held contaminated water usually go. Saving them can cost more later, and we would rather explain that now than after mold appears.

Respect for your home and your time

Crews are asked to protect floors on the way in, to keep work areas contained and to explain what they are moving and why. Water damage is already disruptive, and the work should not add to it. If something about the process is unclear, ask. A good crew would rather explain than leave you guessing.

Training, equipment and records

Restoration relies on measuring, not guessing. Crews use moisture meters to find wet materials, thermal cameras to see behind finishes and drying equipment sized to the room. Readings are recorded at each visit so you and your insurer can see the progress.

Industry practice follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration and S520 for mold. You can ask any company, including us, how those standards shape the plan for your job.

Why restoration is more than cleanup

Mopping up leaves water inside walls and under floors. Restoration removes that hidden water, dries the structure and checks that it is dry. That is the difference between a floor that looks fine for a week and one that stays sound. The pages on structural drying and water extraction explain how.

What a typical call looks like

A typical call starts with a single question: where is the water coming from? From there the dispatcher works through a short list. Is the main closed? Which rooms are wet? Is the water clear, or does it smell? The answers decide which crew and which equipment go out.

Calls are noted with the address and the details, so the crew arrives knowing what to expect. If you are calling for a parent, a tenant or a business, say so. The person at the property may be asked for access, and the dispatcher can explain what to prepare.

After the visit, you get a plain explanation of the scope and the cost, and you decide how to proceed. No one should feel pressed to sign in the middle of a crisis.

We also say when a repair is not ours to promise. Restoration is not waterproofing, so a basement that floods every spring needs a drainage contractor as well. We will tell you that plainly.
