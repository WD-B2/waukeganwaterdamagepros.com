Water Damage in a Rental: What Waukegan Tenants and Landlords Should Know

The first hour matters more than the blame. Tell the landlord in writing, stop the water and take photos.

By the Waukegan Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A leak in a rental creates a three-way problem. The tenant lives with the water, the landlord owns the building and an insurer sits behind each of them. Nothing speeds up a repair like clear communication in the first hour, and nothing slows it down like a dispute about who should have acted first.

This guide is general information for Waukegan tenants and landlords. It is not legal advice, and your lease and local rules decide who is responsible for what.

For tenants: the first hour

  1. Stop the source if you can. Close the valve under the sink or the shutoff behind the appliance, and use the main shutoff only if the lease and building allow it.
  2. Tell the landlord or property manager at once, and put it in writing by text or email as well as a call.
  3. Photograph and record video of the water, the source and your belongings before anything is moved.
  4. Move your belongings out of the wet area, and keep receipts for anything you buy because of the leak.
  5. Call your own renters insurer if your belongings are damaged.

The Illinois Department of Insurance says flood damage is usually not included in homeowners or renters policies, so a renters policy generally will not pay for flood water that came from outside. A sudden pipe failure inside the building is a different category, and you should ask your insurer how your policy treats it.

For landlords: act quickly and keep records

Landlords usually direct the repair of the building, which means arranging the plumber, the drying and the rebuild. Waiting costs money. Drywall and flooring that stay wet longer need more replacement, and a slow leak can reach the unit below.

  • Respond in writing the same day, and tell the tenant what you are doing.
  • Arrange a plumber and a restoration crew rather than waiting for an insurer to schedule an adjuster.
  • Keep moisture logs, photos and invoices for your own insurer.
  • Check whether the damage extends to a neighboring unit.

Multi-unit buildings add complications because water crosses ceilings and walls. A crew that documents each unit separately makes it easier to sort out which policy applies. See commercial water damage restoration for how larger and shared-wall losses are handled.

Mold after a rental leak

If water lingered, growth can follow. Tell the landlord in writing if you see or smell it, and do not try to remove large amounts yourself. The mold remediation page explains the process, and the moisture source must be fixed first.

Waukegan rental notes from primary sources

The Illinois Department of Insurance states that flood damage is usually excluded from homeowners and renters policies and that flood coverage is a separate purchase. Ask your renters insurer about your own policy.

The City of Waukegan building department is the office to ask about code complaints and inspections for your address. We do not quote local rental rules, because the city is the right source and rules change.

The same is true in nearby villages such as Wadsworth and Lake Villa, where the village hall handles building questions.

Keeping the paper trail clean on both sides

Most rental disputes start with a missing record. Tenants should report the problem in writing and keep a copy. A text or email with the date, the room and a photo counts. Landlords should reply in writing too, and note when a contractor was called.

  • Tenants: photograph damaged belongings before moving them and keep receipts for replacements
  • Tenants: write down any days you could not use a room
  • Landlords: log every call, visit and repair with a date
  • Both: keep the drying records so the repair timeline is clear

Local rules and lease terms decide who pays for what, so ask the City of Waukegan or a tenant resource about your situation. If the unit sits in Wadsworth or Lake Villa, ask that village instead. A dry structure protects everyone, which is why structural drying should start early.

If a unit becomes unsafe, tenants should ask in writing about temporary options, and landlords should reply the same way. Written records settle most disagreements later.

Frequently asked questions

Who calls the restoration company in a rental?

Usually the landlord, because they own the building. In an emergency a tenant can call to stop damage, but should tell the landlord immediately.

Does renters insurance cover water damage?

It generally covers your belongings, not the building. Flood is usually excluded. Ask your insurer about sudden plumbing leaks.

Can I stop paying rent if my unit floods?

That depends on your lease and local law. Ask a tenant advocacy group or an attorney before withholding rent.

What if my landlord does not respond?

Keep documenting in writing. The City of Waukegan building department takes code concerns, and a tenant advocacy group can explain your options.

Do I need to move my belongings out?

Move them away from the wet area, and photograph everything first. Keep receipts for storage or replacement.
