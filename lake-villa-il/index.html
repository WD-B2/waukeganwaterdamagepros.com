How the Lake Villa water system shapes a leak

Water damage restoration in Lake Villa, IL starts with the village water system, which changed recently. Lake Villa joined Lake County and the Village of Lindenhurst in 2013 to bring Lake Michigan water to its system, along with Fox Lake Hills and Grandwood Park. Before that, these communities drew from underground aquifers, and the deep aquifer had been depleted.

Construction began in 2016 and finished in four phases in 2019. For a homeowner, the practical result is that a supply line leak inside a house starts with treated lake water, which is the simplest case for cleanup if the water is removed quickly.

When water is spreading in Lake Villa

The steps are about safety first. Stop the source, keep out of danger and let a crew handle the water.

  1. Close the main shutoff if a pipe or appliance is the source.
  2. If water is touching outlets, appliances or the panel, stay clear, and if anything electrical or gas related worries you, phone the utility or 911 first.
  3. If sewage is involved, stop using drains, keep people out of the water and call.
  4. Photograph the water and the damage before anything is moved.

What the village infrastructure means for a sewer backup

Lake Villa public works maintains more than 39 miles of sanitary sewers and 11 wastewater pumping stations, along with more than 44 miles of water mains, according to its own page. The village also runs storm sewers. That matters when a drain backs up. A plumber can tell you whether the blockage is in your house line, and public works can tell you whether the village main is involved.

If sewage reaches a floor, sewage backup cleanup explains the protective-gear process and why porous materials are removed. For a plain pump-out, water extraction describes what happens first.
