Carpet Water Damage in Waukegan, IL

Carpet holds water in the pad and the floor beneath it. What happens next depends on the water source and the hours since it got wet.

(847) 368-7353

Carpet water damage restoration in Waukegan covers extracting water from carpet and pad, drying the floor beneath, and deciding what can be saved. Carpet looks dry on top long before the pad and subfloor are. If your carpet is soaked or has been damp for more than a day, call dispatch and keep furniture legs off the wet surface.

Carpet is dry on top and wet underneath

The carpet fibers dry first. Under them sit a foam or fiber pad and the subfloor, and both hold water for days. People often pull up a corner of carpet to find dampness, a musty smell or a patch of growth on the back, which means the pad stayed wet longer than the surface suggested.

Water sourceTypical outcome for the carpetTypical outcome for the pad
Clean supply line or applianceOften dried and kept if fastOften removed, sometimes dried
Gray water from a washer or dishwasherCleaned and dried case by caseUsually removed
Flood or sewageUsually removedRemoved
Slow leak over daysDried if the carpet is intactOften removed

How crews handle wet carpet

Extraction comes first. A weighted extractor presses water out of the carpet and pad, and a carpet that has been wet only briefly is usually lifted, the pad removed and the carpet dried over air movers. A thermal camera and moisture meter check the subfloor, because water under the carpet can sit against the slab or plywood.

Carpet that is dried in place on its original pad may need lifting at the edges to let air reach the back. Drying follows the steps on the structural drying page. If the water came from a source like a washing machine hose, see appliance leak water damage.

Save, replace or discard

  • Save the carpet when the water was clean, the leak was brief, and it can be lifted and dried with the pad replaced.
  • Replace the pad in most cases, because it is inexpensive and slow to dry.
  • Discard the carpet after contaminated water, such as flood or sewage, because it cannot be sanitized reliably.
  • Replace carpet that has delaminated, stretched beyond re-stretching or developed growth.

If you find mold growth under carpet, see mold remediation. Mold needs moisture, so the water source and the drying come before cleaning growth.

Carpet water damage in Waukegan homes

Carpet is common in bedrooms and finished basements, and the basement is where many wet carpets end up in this area. If your carpet sat over a basement slab, it may have wicked from below after a rain, and our basement water damage page covers that case. Rentals have the same rules. Tell the landlord and keep the date you first saw water.

Practical tie-back: a stain on the ceiling below a carpeted room means water went through the floor. Check the room underneath before you decide the carpet is the only loss.

A sudden leak that soaks carpet is often covered by homeowners insurance, while long-term dampness often is not. Ask your insurer, and keep a sample of the carpet and pad if they are replaced.

Whether carpet and pad can be saved depends on the water category defined in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and on how long the materials stayed wet.

Frequently asked questions

Can wet carpet be saved?

Often, when the water was clean and it is lifted and dried quickly. Carpet that sat in flood or sewage water is normally replaced.

Do I have to replace the pad?

Most of the time. Pad is inexpensive, slow to dry and can hold moisture that leads to odor.

How long until wet carpet grows mold?

It varies with temperature and moisture, so there is no safe number. The sooner it is extracted and dried, the better.

Can I rent a carpet cleaner to dry it?

A household carpet cleaner can remove surface water, but it cannot dry the pad and subfloor. Professional extraction and drying equipment is designed for that.

Is wet carpet covered by insurance?

Often when the cause is a sudden leak. Slow leaks and flood damage are treated differently. Ask your insurer.
